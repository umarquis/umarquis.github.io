I am a PhD student in the [Mathematics department](https://www.maths.unitn.it/) of the University of Trento and in the [Complex Human Behavior Lab](https://chub.fbk.eu/) in FBK. My research focuses on the growth of cities. I combine theoretical modeling, empirical analysis, and computational simulations to study these processes, drawing on tools from statistical physics, nonlinear dynamics, and complex systems.

1) Empirical laws. The amount and the particularity of data concerning cities has exploded. While there has been many observations on the growth of population and economies, the spatial dynamics of cities remain understudied. Using data from satllite imagery, I try to characterize simple laws governing city growth.

2) Science of city. 

3) "Exotic" kinetic roughening. While most of the surface growth literature has focused on the thermal KPZ equation, my work focuses on more exotic cases such as heterogeneous deposition or non-linear geometries. 

4) Traveling waves in random media. Reaction-diffusion equations describe a variety of (non-equilibrium) phenomena: flame propagation, ecological invasion, epidemic spreading etc. I work on characterizing fronts which advance in heterogeneous media and deviations to various deterministic cases.

5) Spatial networks.

6) Random matrix theory (and applications). It appears that laws governing the spectrum of random matrices appear everywhere across science: spin-glasses, random surfaces, econophysics, stability of ecological systems... However the discipline is still young and there are plenty of open questions. I have worked on the spectrum of matrices with strong correlations.

7) Soft matter, biophysics. I am currently discovering these incredibly wide and fascinating disciplines. I am particulary interested in the stretching of polymers and first-passage problems in complex media.
